          Prep once, execute, hand it to the AE

          How an Incode BDR plans the day, opens the call, handles pushback and follows up. It covers cold calling, account research, discovery, AI-enabled prospecting, events and AE partnership.

          30-second pitch. "Incode verifies that a person is real and is who they say they are, in seconds, for banks, telcos, marketplaces and governments. Most of our customers come to us for one of two reasons: AI fraud like deepfakes is getting past their current checks, or good customers are dropping out of sign-up. We already work with companies like HSBC, Klarna, SIXT and TikTok, who use us to cut fraud without adding friction. I was looking to find 30 minutes this week to show you how. Does Thursday or Friday work?"

            Open the call

            The value statement: Who → Why → What

            Give the prospect context before you ask them to engage. Keep the Why to about three short sentences in their language.

            Who

            Who you are

            Your name, Incode, and the team or segment you support.

            Why

            Why you're calling

            Their segment, two or three pains you solve, and peers they know who already use Incode.

            What

            What you want

            A short, specific slot on their calendar, with two days to choose from.

            You're selling a 30-minute meeting, not the whole product.

            Keep an assumptive frame

            Confidence carries a cold call. Lead the next step instead of asking permission.

            Weak frame

              Upward inflection · passive language · prospect has to lead

              Strong frame

                Downward inflection · assumptive language · you lead the next step

                Talk tracks by persona

                Each one follows Who, Why, What. Name peers from the prospect's own industry: a bank cares what HSBC does, not what TikTok does. Save analyst awards for the follow-up email, executives and procurement.

                Handle pushback

                When they object, sell the meeting first

                Don't turn a cold call into a debate about their current setup or the whole product. Stay calm, acknowledge, ask again. After the second pushback, stop and set a dated follow-up.

                First line · ARA

                Acknowledge → Reassert → AdvanceAgree, give one specific reason to meet, then offer two days.

                Second line · ACE

                Ask → Clarify → ExpandOnly if they push back again: ask about the gap, then ask once more.
                Follow up

                Simple beats sophisticated

                The cadence isn't magic; the consistency is. Split each segment into groups A and B and alternate email and call, so every day has a call list and an email list ready.

                Easy to remember, measure and repeat

                1. Email
                2. Call
                3. Email
                4. Call
                5. …for up to 8 touches, then a short break-up email and move to nurture

                Adapted for enterprise buyers

                • Calls can run daily: most go unanswered and nobody notices a missed call.
                • Emails are capped at 2 a week per person. On a third email day, call instead.
                • LinkedIn is a light layer (view, react), not a step.

                AI prompt kit for prospecting

                Use AI to speed up research and personalization. Paste these into Claude with a company or person filled in. Always check facts before using them on a call.

                Account research

                Research [Company] for an identity verification sales call. Give me: what they sell and to whom, recent news (funding, launches, fraud incidents, regulatory actions) from the last 12 months, which regulations likely apply (KYC, AML, age verification), who probably owns fraud, compliance and security, and the single best trigger to mention on a cold call. Flag anything you are unsure of.

                Value statement

                Write a cold call value statement for [Name], [Title] at [Company], in three parts. Who: one line introducing me from Incode. Why: at most three short sentences naming their segment, two pains Incode solves for it (deepfakes, sign-up drop-off, KYC compliance or help-desk fraud) and one or two Incode customers from their industry. What: an assumptive ask for 30 minutes this week offering two specific days. Sound like a person, not a script.

                Email that gets replies

                Write a cold email under 90 words to [Name], [Title] at [Company]. Subject line under 5 words. Open with their likely problem, add one proof point, and end with a soft question, not a meeting link. For the proof point, name 1 or 2 Incode customers from their industry (e.g. HSBC for banks, Klarna for fintech, PokerStars for gaming), not an award. No buzzwords.

                Call recap for the AE

                Turn these call notes into a handoff for my Account Executive: [notes]. Format: company and contact, the pain in their words, BANT status (budget, authority, need, timing) with evidence, objections raised and how I handled them, agreed next step and date, and suggested angle for the first meeting.

                Account tiering

                Account research and prioritization: spend most calls on Tier A.

                Tier A · call this week

                • Regulated industry with high sign-up volume (bank, fintech, telco, gaming, marketplace)
                • A trigger: fraud incident, new regulation, app launch, funding, vendor renewal
                • A named fraud, compliance or security leader you can reach

                Tier B · work in cadence

                • Right industry and size, but no trigger yet
                • Contact known but not the decision maker
                • Nurture with content: case studies, Deepfakes 101

                Tier C · park

                • Low volume or no regulatory pressure
                • Looking for a free or self-serve tool
                • Revisit each quarter or after a trigger

                Event and networking plan

                For Money20/20, Decoded and industry dinners.

                Before

                Book meetings early

                • Pull the attendee and speaker list; tier the accounts
                • Email Tier A two weeks out: invite them to the booth or the Deepfake Guesser
                • Agree with your AE who covers which accounts
                During

                Start conversations

                • Use the Deepfake Guesser as the opener: "Can you spot the fake?"
                • Ask one discovery question, take a photo of the badge
                • Log each conversation in your CRM the same day
                After

                Follow up in 48 hours

                • Personal email referencing what you talked about
                • Connect on LinkedIn with a note
                • Book the meeting or set a dated next step
